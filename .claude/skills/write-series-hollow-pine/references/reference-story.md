## The story as told (Episode 1, 0:00–1:31)

### Act 1: The Lie Before the Lie (0:00–0:13 / 0–14%)

The video opens cold: a teenage girl (char_01) stumbles through fog-shrouded pine woods at night,
blood at her nose and mouth, clutching her jacket shut. Her voiceover cuts in flat and bitter:
"They call this place a school. Remember that. It's the first lie." She stops against a tree, scans
the dark, and calls a name into the fog — "Nora? Is that you?" (0:07–0:09). No one answers on screen.

At 0:09.5 the image hard-cuts to black. A title card stamps the frame: "HOLLOW PINE" /
"48 HOURS EARLIER." A rewind-icon graphic plays over a desaturated black-and-white shot of a school
bus full of sleeping students — the protagonist is on it, fidgeting with a ring. Her VO continues over
the transition: "The brochure says paradise." Everything we just saw is now revealed as a
flash-forward; the real story starts two days earlier, on this bus.

**Turning point (9.53s):** the title card reframes the cold open and resets the timeline.

### Act 2: Rules, a Photo, and a Lie (0:13–0:56 / 14–62%)

The bus stops at a rusted iron gate deep in the pines, its bars scored with deep claw marks. A stern
caretaker (char_03) in an olive field jacket meets the students with a wooden crate. "Phones," he
says. "All of them." The protagonist's VO is dry: "It means rich parents paying a fortune to throw
their kids away politely." She surrenders her phone like everyone else and passes through the
padlocked gate.

Inside, in a lamp-lit, wood-paneled assembly hall under a carved "RULES" sign, an elderly,
cane-leaning headmaster (char_04) recites the school's laws in a flat, ominous voice: no leaving
rooms after lights out, no restricted zones, no woods. Then the one that matters most — "Rule six:
you are responsible for each other's lives. If one of you dies here, all of you answer for it." The
protagonist's VO immediately undercuts him: "Rule six isn't about safety, binding 13 wrists." She
sees the rule for what it is — control dressed as care.

A single held shot (12.5 seconds, the longest take of the episode) carries us from a smirking young
teacher (char_07) flipping a silver coin, into a school hallway, where the protagonist stops at an old
sepia photograph. A nameplate reads "ELI." Scratched crudely into the frame: "STILL HERE." Her VO
finally tells the truth she's been hiding from everyone else: "The others were sent here to be
forgotten. I came for the one they erased." A classmate (char_05) notices her staring and asks, "You
know him?" She lies without hesitating: "No, just an old picture."

That night, rain streaks the dormitory window. Her roommate (char_02) shakes her awake — "Shh." — and
together they watch figures moving in the dark schoolyard below, heading for the woods. "And that one
saw us watching," the roommate whispers. The decision is made without a word: they go too.

**Turning point (51.99s):** she and her roommate spot students sneaking toward the woods.

### Act 3: The Voice From the Trees (0:56–1:31 / 62–100%)

Outside, the protagonist leads her roommate after the others: "They went west. Into the part of the
woods where the trees are dead." A blonde student leader (char_06) rallies the group in the
headmaster's own language: "Six gone, six here. If one of them is lost out there, we all answer for
it." Her VO notes the irony with contempt: "One night in, and they've already learned to look for
someone to blame." Rule Six, stated as policy that same morning, is already a weapon in student hands.

Standing close to char_05, the protagonist admits something she's told no one: "That voice. Six years
I've heard that voice in my sleep." He's startled — "You've heard it before." — which implies he may
have too. "Tonight it came from the trees," she says. The group argues about waiting until morning;
she refuses: "If we wait for daylight, whoever called out might be gone... I need to know what."
Voices in the dark murmur that someone is "moving something down there... in the middle of the night."

They reach the edge of the woods, facing a house with a single lit window — and a silhouette watching
them from inside. The episode's final beat is internal: her VO, cold and certain, "Whoever I follow
tonight becomes the one who knows what I am." An unidentified voice, half dream and half warning,
answers: "Choose carefully."

The screen holds on her face — afraid, but no longer hesitating — and ends there, unresolved.

**Turning point (84.07s):** the choice is framed as irreversible and left unresolved.
